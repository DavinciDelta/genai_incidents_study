# Step 5 — What attackers did, and the mitigations that address it (draft, pre-coding)

Generated by `s05_techniques.py` (`make s05`); do not edit by hand. The design follows Hamer et al., *Closing the Chain* (arXiv:2503.12192): collect what attackers did, map it to the framework tasks that mitigate it, rank the tasks, and list what the framework leaves uncovered. Three things differ from that paper and limit what these tables can claim:

- **Labels are not coded from reports.** The corpus assigns OWASP categories by keyword rules, then computes most ATLAS techniques from them with a fixed lookup. RQ1 therefore reports OWASP categories, the layer the rest is derived from, next to a human check of the same records.
- **OWASP categories are kinds of risk, not techniques.** They say what kind of weakness or harm a record was filed under, not how the attacker operated. Technique-level claims wait for the hand-coding phase.
- **One mapping chain, not four independent strategies.** RQ2 runs OWASP category → corpus lookup → ATLAS technique → ATLAS mitigation, using MITRE's own `mitigates` relationships in ATLAS 2026.06. There is no LLM or report-derived mapping to triangulate against.

How to read the tables: a record can carry several categories or techniques, so their percentages are shares of records carrying the label and do not sum to 100. Percentages are rounded to one decimal unless a column says otherwise. ON-AI and WITH-AI are separate populations and are never pooled; 95% CIs are percentile bootstrap intervals over records (2,000 resamples). Category numbers (LLM01 …) are the corpus's; the project that supplied the human labels numbers the same ten categories differently, so the two are matched here by name.

## What these tables cannot say

- **Whether a mitigation is implemented.** Nothing in the corpus records which controls a victim had in place. The category and lifecycle columns describe who would have to act and when; adoption needs evidence from outside the corpus.
- **How attackers operated.** OWASP categories are kinds of risk and the ATLAS labels are mostly computed from them. Prevalence follows the corpus's rules and its disclosure channels; the channel and human-check columns are there so each row can be discounted accordingly.
- **Chains.** A record's labels are an unordered set, so nothing here orders them into an attack sequence.
