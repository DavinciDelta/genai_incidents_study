## Use cases

- *Deployers.* Improper Output Handling's ON-AI +11 is rule output: 238 of 548 records carry a code-injection seed vector, 5% demonstrations (Table 5.1).
- *Researchers.* Prompt Injection, the vote's first, ranks 3rd in ON-AI; 17 MCP Tool Interface Exploitation rows (14 corpus-filed as Supply Chain Vulnerabilities) seed sampling (Table 5.3).
- *Framework authors.* 74 of 174 WITH-AI hand labels fit no entry and Weaponized LLM Abuse, 43 labels, has no corpus code: deepfake fraud has no LLM mechanism (Table 5.3).

**Not supported:** prevalence; a corrected Top 10; attack chains; trends; attribution or exploitation status; statements about how attackers operated.

## Limitations of the data

- Labels are rule outputs: 36.0% of OWASP codes come from the attack_vector seed, the rest from ingest records (Tables 5.1, 5.2).
- Supply Chain Vulnerabilities marks the CVE channel and 604 of 686 cve/ghsa frame records are ON-AI, so pooled comparisons compare channels (Tables 1.10, 2.2).
- One coder hand-labelled a quota sample: 161 ON-AI and 174 WITH-AI rows (Tables 1.14, 5.3).
- The split is rule-based: 707 of 1,262 vector-only WITH-AI rows name no adversary (Table 2.5).
- Join losses: 77 of 1,200 hand-labelled rows have no current record of their own (Table 1.13).
- Vote–data concordance is weak: weighted κ 0.20 (−0.16 to 0.57) (Table 1.15).
- The two OWASP numberings differ on 8 of ten entries, so joins use names (Table 1.0).
- Record year, date precision, stubs and empty fields: steps 1, 2 and 4.
