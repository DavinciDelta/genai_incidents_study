# Step 5 — OWASP categories per population, where a human reader and the community vote disagree with the corpus, and the ATLAS mitigations the labels map to (draft before hand-coding)

Generated by `s05_techniques.py` (`make s05`); do not edit by hand. Conventions, 95% CI, pooled rows and the glossary: `out/s01_overview.md` section 0.

The design follows Hamer et al., *Closing the Chain* (arXiv:2503.12192): collect what attackers did, map it to the framework tasks that mitigate it, rank the tasks, and list what the framework leaves uncovered. Here the rows are OWASP LLM Top 10 (2026) categories, which are kinds of risk, not techniques: they say what a record was filed under, not how the attacker operated. The corpus assigns them by keyword rules and then derives most of its ATLAS technique labels from them with a fixed lookup (76.0% of ATLAS labels on ON-AI records and 87.9% on WITH-AI records are what that lookup produces from the record's own OWASP codes), so the categories are the layer everything else rests on. The 33 BOTH records are excluded. WITH-AI leaves after Table 5.7: neither OWASP nor ATLAS is written for the case where the AI is the attacker's instrument. The technique → mitigation links are MITRE's own, from ATLAS 2026.06; nothing is mapped by a model. Names: Tables 5.1, 5.2, 5.4 and 5.5 use rank-validation's entry names (Table 1.0); Tables 5.3 and 5.6–5.9 use the corpus's.

## Use cases

What the tables support today, each with its caveat.

- *Deployers of AI systems.* Table 5.1 shows, per disclosure channel, which categories the corpus labelling places above the community vote (Improper Output Handling: corpus 2nd, vote 13th) and Table 5.4 where a human reader kept or changed the corpus's label. Table 5.8 shows Generative AI Guardrails reaching 66–100% of ON-AI records per channel; Table 5.9 shows that the two most frequent technique labels (command and scripting interpreter 45%, valid accounts 25%) have no ATLAS mitigation. Caveat: labels are keyword rules, and Supply Chain marks the CVE channel (98%).
- *Researchers.* Table 5.3 shows which categories the index sees realized but rarely demonstrated (Improper Output Handling 5%, Supply Chain 7%) and the reverse (Unbounded Consumption 60%, Hidden Context Exposure 56%, Data and Model Poisoning 52%); Table 4.6 and the unstated rows of Tables 4.1–4.5 size the coding work. Caveat: 'demonstrated' tracks the research channel, 3% of the corpus.
- *Framework authors.* Table 5.5: 74 of 174 hand-labelled WITH-AI records fit none of 20 entries, mostly deepfake fraud and abuse imagery; Table 5.6: one lookup link produces every AML.T0058 Publish Poisoned Models label on ON-AI records; Table 5.2: Weaponized LLM Abuse absorbs 43 hand labels with no corpus code.
- *Database maintainers.* Table 4.6: `mitigations` and `impact` are filled on 4.4% of ON-AI and 0.4% of WITH-AI records, `discovery_method` on 55 of 15,666 records; Table 1.10: OWASP codes mark channels.

**Not supported:** prevalence in the world; a corrected or human-ranked Top 10; attack chains or sequences; mitigation adoption or efficacy; trends over time; attribution or exploitation status; technique-level statements about how attackers operated.

## Limitations of the data

genai_incidents is an index of other trackers in which every label is the output of a rule, so this study uses it to find and stratify incidents, never to measure them. The items below are ordered by how much each threatens the study's claims; each carries the number that bounds the damage and the table that reports it.

1. **Channel confounding.** Population tracks disclosure channel (cve/ghsa 604 of 686 frame records ON-AI; harm-db 1,370 of 1,753 WITH-AI; research/other 365 of 393 ON-AI), as do description length (median 557 / 263 / 259 characters), date precision, demonstrated share and the end of the record series (harm-db stops 2026-07, cve/ghsa runs to 2026-10). Any pooled ON/WITH ratio or cross-population comparison on description-derived fields compares channels (Tables 2.2, 1.6, 1.7, 2.10).
2. **Labels are rule outputs.** 36.0% of OWASP LLM code assignments equal the code seeded from `attack_vector` (WITH-AI 61.2%), and 90.7% of ATLAS technique labels (ON-AI 76.0%, WITH-AI 87.9%; ON-AI research/other 56%) are reproducible from the build script's OWASP→ATLAS lookup, an upper bound on lookup-derived labels. The corpus ranks in Tables 5.1–5.2 and the mitigation ranking in Table 5.8 rank keyword rules, not observed techniques (Table 5.6).
3. **Supply Chain is a channel marker.** 7,917 of 8,063 CVE/GHSA records (98.2%) carry corpus LLM04 regardless of attack vector (xss 1,335, other 1,332, auth-bypass 1,190), arriving with the upstream feed; within ON-AI it sits on 77.2% of cve/ghsa and 10.3% of harm-db records. Its corpus rank 1 in ON-AI is a channel artefact (Tables 1.10, 5.1).
4. **The split is rule-based.** 707 of 1,262 WITH-AI rows assigned by the corpus vector alone (48.4% of WITH-AI) contain no adversary word in the searched text; 80 WITH-AI rows are CVE/GHSA records; 285 conventional-exploit rows enter ON-AI on security-register words; 7,404 vulnerability disclosures are gated to NONE. Population sizes carry an unmeasured false-positive and false-negative rate (Table 2.5).
5. **The hand-labelled set is a quota sample from LLM pre-labels, by one coder.** 1,200 rows: up to 30 unanimous and 30 majority rows per LLM-consensus entry (660 rows; 7 entries reach the cap of 60), all 431 rows with no majority, and 109 rows whose pre-label majority was 'no entry'; one person who saw the three LLM votes after a blind read (blind label = final label on 67.4%); only 38 of the 1,200 are in rank-validation's `samples.json`, and the cycle marks itself NON-PUBLISHABLE. Hand-labelled counts support 'where labellings disagree' and the existence of no-entry rows, never a ranking or prevalence (Tables 1.14, 5.1–5.2).
6. **No-entry shares describe the sample.** 62 of the 74 WITH-AI no-entry rows are disagree-tier rows, a tier taken in full (105 of 105) while 6–9% of other rows were taken; no-entry runs 59.0% / 22.5% / 10.3% across the disagree / split / agree tiers. The 42.5% is not the share of WITH-AI records that fit no entry, and no CI is attached (Table 5.5).
7. **Join losses and merged duplicates.** Of 1,200 hand-labelled rows 55 no longer resolve to a current id and 1,145 land on 1,123 records (22 collapse onto records already counted; 7 records received two or more hand labels and 6 of them disagreeing ones, of which the record's own label, else the first in rank-validation's file order, is kept; Tables 5.1–5.2 name those in each population); of 6,639 classifier rows 240 do not resolve and 192 collapse. Rank-validation covers 52.4% of ON-AI and 70.2% of WITH-AI (37.6% of frame records dated 2026), so its columns describe the pre-May-2026 part of each population (Table 1.13).
8. **The data rank is a whole-snapshot statistic with weak concordance.** Of the 6,207 joined records 69.4% are NONE and 27.8% ON-AI or WITH-AI (28.3% with BOTH, Table 1.13); weighted κ = 0.20 (−0.16, 0.57) over 17 of 20 entries; 3 entries are frame-blind; 17 of 20 data-rank intervals (15 of the 17 with a data rank) span 10 or more places, and the ai-harm records have no precision measurement. Vote-vs-data is the other project's finding, used for expectation-setting only (Table 1.15).
9. **OWASP numbering mismatch.** 8 of the ten entries carry different codes in the corpus and in rank-validation (corpus LLM04 Supply Chain = rv LLM03; corpus LLM07 Misinformation = rv LLM09; corpus LLM10 Improper Output Handling = rv LLM05), and rank-validation's quick-reference names for the proposed entries are stale. Every join is by name and every table prints names (Table 1.0).
10. **Record year is ingestion.** 7,401 of 15,213 records dated 2019 or later (48.6%) carry 2026; all records were added in 4 added-months of 2026, 14 distinct `added` dates (2026-05 7,468, 2026-06 4,722, 2026-07 1,171, 2026-10 2,305; `first_seen` equals `added` by construction); harm-database records end 2026-07-16 while CVE/GHSA records run to 2026-10 (ON-AI has 130 CVE/GHSA rows after the last harm-db month, WITH-AI 2). No table is a trend (Tables 1.5, 1.6, 2.11).
11. **Date precision.** ON-AI dates are 74.2% month-only (CVE rows), WITH-AI 77.5% day-level and 14.2% year-only, so the populations cannot be placed on a common axis finer than a year (Tables 1.7, 2.10).
12. **Descriptions are short and partly stubs.** Median 281 characters (ON-AI 295, WITH-AI 268); 193 WITH-AI records (13.2%) are AIAAIC facts-only stubs (`description_provenance == 'original'`, median 107 characters) that defeat every keyword rule; 535 titles end in an ellipsis. The corpus is a frame; coding is from primary references (Tables 4.4b, 4.6).
13. **CVE records are coded as conventional weaknesses.** 45.1% of ON-AI carries a CVE or CWE; the top ON-AI CWEs are CWE-506, CWE-94, CWE-918, CWE-78, CWE-22; CWE-1427 (prompt injection) appears on 3 corpus records while 132 ON-AI prompt-injection CVEs carry CWE-94, CWE-22, CWE-77, CWE-78. Step 4's 'CVE/CWE present' is an upper bound on 'needed a software bug' (Table 4.3).
14. **ATLAS gaps are partly framework artefacts.** ATLAS 2026.06 mitigates 89 of 173 techniques and 13 of 52 ATT&CK-adapted techniques in ATLAS; 38.7% of ON-AI technique labels sit on unmitigated techniques (1,072 on ATT&CK-adapted ones, counting a sub-technique's parent reference) and 63.6% of WITH-AI labels are Impact outcomes. Table 5.9 mixes 'ATLAS has not imported ATT&CK mitigations' with 'no known mitigation', and nothing records whether a victim had any control.
15. **Rule-based dimensions have large 'unstated' shares and known defects.** Entry point unstated 48.8% of ON-AI, target 27.0%, AI medium 48.8% of WITH-AI, objective 38.8%; substring defects are listed in Table 4.7 with before/after counts. Every step 4 share is a lower bound and the complement is the hand-coding workload (Tables 4.1b, 4.4b, 4.7).
16. **Exploitation, attribution and housekeeping fields are nearly empty.** `exploited_in_wild` is true on 14 records (3 ON-AI), `discovery_method` on 55, `mitigations` on 59 ON-AI and 6 WITH-AI records (as recommendations, not controls the victim had); 29 retracted records remain (2 in ON-AI); 1,340 multi-source records are counted under their first-match channel. Realized vs demonstrated rests on the record-type field alone (Table 4.6).
