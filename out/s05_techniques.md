## Why 43% of the hand-labelled WITH-AI records fit no category

74 of 174 hand-labelled WITH-AI records (42.5%) fit no entry. Design: the sample took every record the three LLM pre-labellers disagreed on (105 of 174) but only 6–9% of the other rows; 62 of the 74 are such rows, and no entry runs 59% / 22% / 10% across the disagree / two agree / three agree tiers. Substance: every rubric entry requires an LLM mechanism, which deepfake fraud and abuse imagery lack: the 148 deepfake records among the 174 split 61 no entry / 42 Misinformation / 39 Weaponized LLM Abuse / 6 other. The share describes this sample only.

## Use cases

- *Deployers.* ON-AI: Improper Output Handling +11 places (corpus-coded on 11 of 13); MCP Tool Interface Exploitation 17 hand labels, no code (filed as Supply Chain Vulnerabilities 14); Excessive Agency corpus-coded on 3 of 9.
- *Researchers.* Data and Model Poisoning: most-used hand label (22), corpus-coded on 13 of 22; Prompt Injection: best carried outside channel markers (14 of 15); Improper Output Handling: realized, rarely demonstrated (5%).
- *Framework authors.* 74 of 174 WITH-AI hand labels fit no entry and Weaponized LLM Abuse (43 hand labels) has no code: deepfake fraud is outside the list's LLM-mechanism scope.
- *Database maintainers.* Persistent Memory Poisoning, MCP Tool Interface Exploitation, Cross-Modal Safety Bypass and Weaponized LLM Abuse need a code (5+ hand labels).

**Not supported:** prevalence; a corrected Top 10; attack chains; mitigations, their adoption or efficacy; trends; attribution or exploitation status; technique-level statements about how attackers operated.

## Limitations of the data

genai_incidents is an index of other trackers in which every label is a rule's output, so this study finds and stratifies incidents with it, never measures them; rows 1–6 bound this step's tables, rows 7–10 the study as a whole (this file runs last; row 10 bundles six stated in full in steps 1, 2 and 4).
